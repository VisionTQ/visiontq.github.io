## Page 15

The figures of rhetoric are the deliberate and artful ways of using language. As Mark Forsyth puts it, they are "the techniques for making a single phrase striking and memorable... not by saying something different, but by saying something in a different way." They are the "formulas for producing great lines."

## Words I Didn't Know

### chap

A chap called George Puttenham had a bestseller in 1589 with his book on them (that's about the year of Shakespeare's first play).

**Definition:** **A Man or Boy:** Primarily used in British English and informal contexts, it means a fellow, guy, or friendly male.

My Example: He is my most trusted chap

I know it doesn't sound natural.

but this is a better example:

He's a good chap

### minuscule

One minuscule part of this massive subject is the figures of rhetoric, which are the techniques for making a single phrase striking and memorable just by altering the wording.

**Definition:** **Minuscule** (adjective) means extremely small, tiny, or negligible in size or amount.
